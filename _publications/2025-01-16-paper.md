Protein phosphorylation is essential for cerebellar development, but the contribution of protein phosphatases remains less well understood. In this study, we investigated the role of protein phosphatase 2A catalytic subunit alpha (PP2Acα) in cerebellar granule neurons using conditional *Ppp2ca* knockout mouse models.

Granule neuron-specific loss of PP2Acα disrupted cerebellar development by impairing granule neuron proliferation, cell-cycle progression, and migration, and by altering Purkinje cell organization. These developmental defects were associated with reduced cerebellar size and deficits in motor coordination, balance, and cognitive performance.

Integrated phosphoproteomic and proteomic analyses further revealed that PP2Acα regulates multiple phosphorylation-dependent neuronal programs, including cell-cycle control, cytoskeletal remodeling, mRNA processing, metabolism, translation, and synaptic signaling. These findings establish PP2Acα as a key regulator of cerebellar development and provide mechanistic insight into phosphatase-related neurodevelopmental disorders.
